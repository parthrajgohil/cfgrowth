# Parthraj's own writing: voice examples

Provided by the CEO on 2026-09-25. Names anonymised; spelling fixed (the originals had
typos). Imitate the **tone**, not the structure. Structure rules still come from
`cold-email` and `linkedin-post`.

## What makes it sound like Parthraj
- **Warm and respectful:** starts from genuine appreciation of the other person's work.
- **Humble, never pushy:** "if you think there is a need…", "let me know".
- **Two easy ways to respond:** a real collaboration *or* just a casual technical chat.
  Keep this two-option CTA; it's his signature move.
- **Courteous, human close:** "Have a nice day!" / "Feel free to reach out."
- **First person, plain words, short sentences.** No jargon for its own sake.
- **LinkedIn:** a hook line, then "Here is why ->", one-line paragraphs, human themes, and a
  takeaway as the last line.

## What NOT to copy from the originals
- Opening with self-introduction ("I am CEO of…, we are based in India…"). Lead with
  *their* product and trigger; put who we are in one line after.
- Vague praise ("a real idea that would help the industry"). Name the specific thing.
- Apologetic filler ("Thank you for your time reading this").
- Long first emails. Cold-email limits still apply (50–110 words).

## Blend: how an agent email should sound
Specific trigger and technical insight (from the research) **plus** Parthraj's warmth and
two-option CTA. For example:

> Hi {{First Name}},
>
> I saw the MagicBox launch with Easee. Making any OCPP wallbox offline-capable is a
> genuinely useful idea for fleets. With the HUMAX charger landing the same year, I
> imagine firmware bandwidth is tight.
>
> I'm the CEO of CoreFragment; we build embedded firmware and hardware for connected
> devices (EFR32, ESP32, BLE, Zigbee, Modbus), under NDA with full IP transfer.
>
> If extra hands on firmware would help, I'd be happy to talk. Or if you'd just enjoy
> a casual chat about offline-first charging tech, I'm up for that too.
>
> Have a nice day!
> Parthraj

## Example 1: first outreach (cold, via LinkedIn/email)

> Hello [First name],
>
> I checked your upcoming product info on LinkedIn and I must say, it is a real idea that
> would help the industry. I am reaching out to you to build a reference and connection.
> I am CEO of CoreFragment Technologies. We are based in India. We are a product
> development service provider company with expertise in embedded firmware and hardware.
>
> If you think there is a need to accelerate the development and we can potentially
> collaborate, let me know. I would be happy to discuss some more. Or, if you'd just like
> to have a casual chat on what we do and talk about the technology, I am up for that as
> well.
>
> Thank you for your time reading this. Have a nice day!

## Example 2: follow-up after a first call

> Hello [First name],
>
> It was really a pleasure to connect with you and discuss your product requirements.
> Whenever you get time, please share the NDA document to sign so that we can continue
> with a more detailed discussion about the tasks to perform.
>
> Feel free to reach out in case of any queries or questions.

## Example 3: LinkedIn post

> The best team-building activity doesn't need a budget.
> It just needs eating together. Here is why ->
>
> When people eat together, conversations become human, not transactional.
>
> No agenda. No presentations.
>
> Just people sharing food, stories, ideas, and the occasional joke.
>
> Some of the strongest teams aren't built during offsites or expensive retreats.
>
> They're built over everyday lunches where titles disappear and people get to know each
> other beyond their roles.
>
> If your team always eats alone at their desks, you're missing one of the simplest ways
> to build trust.
>
> Sometimes the biggest productivity boost starts with everyone sitting at the same table.
