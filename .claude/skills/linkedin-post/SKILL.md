# LinkedIn post rules

**Goal:** get CTOs and founders of hardware products to recognize their own problem
and see CoreFragment as the people who've solved it. Posts should bring leads,
not just likes.

## Structure (120–250 words)

1. **Hook (1–2 lines, shown before "see more"):** a concrete engineering situation
   or a counter-intuitive claim.
   e.g. "Your ESP32 prototype hit 40 hours of battery. Your production nRF52 build
   will not behave the same way, and the reason isn't the chip."
2. **Body:** one idea, explained with real specifics. Use short paragraphs (1–3 lines)
   and line breaks. A short list is fine; don't make the whole post bullets.
3. **Takeaway:** one sentence the reader can act on.
4. **Soft CTA (optional):** invite a comment or DM, or link a blog post in the
   first comment rather than the post itself.

## Content pillars (rotate)

- Engineering decisions: chip, RTOS or protocol trade-offs (from the blog library)
- Lessons from projects, anonymized
- Prototype-to-production pitfalls: cost, BOM, certification, OTA
- AI in devices: edge AI, CV, and voice
- Founder perspective: running a product engineering firm from Ahmedabad for
  global clients

## Rules

- 0–1 emoji, and 3 hashtags at most, at the end.
- No engagement bait ("Agree?", "Comment YES").
- Write in first person as Parthraj unless the draft is for the company page.
- Always output 2 hook variants so the human can pick one.
