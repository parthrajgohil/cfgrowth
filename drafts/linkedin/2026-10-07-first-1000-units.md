## Post

Your prototype works on the bench.
That tells you little about unit 1,000. Here is why ->

A prototype is built, flashed and debugged by the people who designed it.

A production unit is built, programmed and tested by people who have never seen the design.

So before a board goes to its first production run, we lock six things:

1. Test points. On the line, a fixture tests every board. No test pad, no test.

2. Programming and keys. How firmware, serial number and per-device keys reach each unit, and when the debug port gets locked.

3. The BOM. Lifecycle status, and a second source for every part that could stop the line.

4. The bootloader. It ships once and rarely changes. Partition layout, rollback and the OTA path are decided now.

5. EMC pre-compliance. A pre-scan before the formal lab test, while a fix is still cheap.

6. Traceability. Serial number to board revision, firmware version and component lots. When one unit fails in the field, you need to know which others share its parts.

None of these show up in a demo. All of them show up in production.

Lock them while the design is still on the bench.

1. **Hook:** "Works on the bench." / "Ready for 1,000?" Diagram: one prototype board → arrow with orange "?" → grid of 20 boards labelled "1,000 units". "Six things to lock before your first production run."
2. **1 Test points: No test pad, no test.** Fixture pins on test pads, test firmware checks rails, radio, sensors in one pass; adding pads after layout freeze means a respin. Lock: test-pad list, fixture design, pass/fail limits.
3. **2 Programming and keys: Every unit needs its own identity.** Firmware, serial, per-device keys at the factory; lock the debug port (STM32 RDP, nRF52 APPROTECT). Lock: where keys are generated, who holds them, when debug is locked.
4. **3 The BOM: One missing part stops the line.** Lead times and end-of-life notices; lifecycle status Active / NRND / EOL. Lock: lifecycle check, second source or alternate footprint.
5. **4 Bootloader and OTA: The bootloader ships once.** A wrong bootloader means a recall, not an update; partition layout is equally hard to change. Lock: partition layout, image signing, rollback, OTA path.
6. **5 EMC pre-compliance: Scan before the lab does.** Pre-scan before booked lab time; FCC Part 15, IEC 60601-1-2, IEC 61000-6 series. Lock: target standards, pre-scan results, margin.
7. **6 Traceability: Know which units share the fault.** Serial → board revision, firmware version, component lots; ISO 13485 traceability for medical. Lock: serial format, label, end-of-line test record.
8. **Takeaway: Lock it while it's on the bench.** Numbered recap of the six; "None of these show up in a demo. All of them show up in production." Orange pill: "Follow CoreFragment for more".
