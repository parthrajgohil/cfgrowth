Hi,

Your brief for the ESP32-S3 network controller is the kind of board I like working on: parts settled, numbered requirements, a review gate after each package. I design ESP32 hardware and firmware for connected products and have done embedded work for more than ten years.

[YOUR LINE: an ESP32-module board you designed, whether you did the schematic, the layout or both, and how you've worked with Altium file-based SchLib/PcbLib libraries]

On the 48 V hot-plug: a few metres of cable is an inductor, and plugging it into low-ESR ceramics makes it ring, so the input can briefly see close to twice the supply. I'd design for that, not just clamp it: a bulk electrolytic at the connector whose ESR damps the ringing, a TVS with standoff above the max input, reverse protection with an ideal-diode or P-FET stage, inrush limiting ahead of the bulk caps, and a buck rated well above 48 V. I'd also make sure the RS-485 transceivers survive 48 V landing on A/B, because someone on site will miswire a terminal block. The calculations go in the design notes.

Rough hours at my rate, assuming the plan is as complete as you describe: schematic 20-25, placement 10-14, routing and release 25-35, bring-up support 10-15 if you want it. I can start this week, with the schematic ready for your first review in about a week.

One question: do the switched outputs need per-output current limiting with fault reporting to the ESP32, or is a fuse per output enough?

Happy to talk it through on a short call here on Upwork.
