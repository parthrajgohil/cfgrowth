## Cover letter (in a ``` block, exactly the text that will be submitted)
```
Hi,

I read your brief for the 200 W converter board with the STM32G491, and it's work I know well: power design and STM32 boards taken from block diagram to Altium release, in reviewed phases like yours.

The first thing I'd pin down in phase 1 is the low end of "0-60 V". With an 18 V output, anything below that needs a 4-switch buck-boost, and at 200 W the input current climbs fast as the input drops (over 20 A at 9 V). So the minimum input at full power, or a derating curve below some voltage, decides the controller, the FETs and the inductor.

The other early call is the MCU supply. To keep the G491 talking SMBus with the output off, I'd run it from a small wide-input regulator on the input rail rather than from the 18 V output, with a hard enable on the converter controller. Input protection then has to clamp above 60 V without interfering with normal operation.

I've done electronics and firmware for one client continuously since 2016, and my hardware work covers power design, battery management and multilayer STM32 boards. You'd get the full Altium sources and own everything.

Since this goes to turnkey PCBA, I'd also scrub the BOM against your yearly volume, not the prototype quantity, and check each part's end of life. I'm happy to quote each phase as a fixed price once the block diagram is accepted.

One question: what's the realistic minimum input voltage at which you still need the full 200 W?

Happy to talk it through on a short call here on Upwork.

Thanks,
