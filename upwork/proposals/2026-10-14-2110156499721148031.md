## Cover letter
```
Hi,

I read your brief for the three-button vibration device, and it's a good spec to review: you've already separated what you need from what the contractor should decide, which most RFQs don't. My engineering company designs small battery-powered products from PCB and firmware through enclosure and prototypes, so I know what a bidder needs in order to quote properly.

One gap I'd flag straight away is MicroPython plus low power. To get hours of battery life the processor has to sleep between uses, and on most MicroPython boards waking from deep sleep means restarting the interpreter, which can take long enough to miss the first press of a triple press. The RFQ should say how fast the device must respond after sitting idle, and let bidders choose between light sleep, a different MCU or a small hardware wake circuit. Similar gaps hide in "customer reprogrammable" (USB port, charging and the enclosure opening it needs) and in shipping a LiPo product.

My plan follows your milestones: the gap review first, then the RFQ, then the final package with bid channels and a standard quote format. I'd keep the RFQ vendor-neutral. If you'd like, I can also quote the working prototype as a separate milestone once the requirements are settled.

Which MicroPython board is your current version running on?

Happy to talk it through on a short call here on Upwork.

Thanks,
Parthraj
```
