## Cover letter
```
Hi,

I read your brief for the ultra-thin Bluetooth tracking sticker, and starting with a paid feasibility study before any POC is exactly how I'd want to run it. I've worked with Nordic BLE chips for the last eight years.

My first concern isn't the chip. It's the battery and the antenna. The thinnest cells that fit a sticker struggle with the short current peaks of each BLE advertisement, so battery type, a buffer capacitor and the advertising interval decide thickness and battery life together. And because the sticker goes on whatever the user chooses, its antenna detunes differently on plastic, wood, a laptop lid or metal. I'd measure range on those surfaces in the first week.

Find My needs an early look too. As I understand Apple's accessory rules, a tag must be able to play a sound for anti-stalking alerts, and a buzzer may set the minimum size more than the radio does. Happy to talk it through on a short call here on Upwork.
```

## Screening answers
1. Miniature BLE devices, completed examples:
   Yes, small BLE devices are most of my work. The closest examples: firmware and iOS/Android apps for a BLE smart glucometer built on a Laird BLE module, a BLE ePaper display device, and nRF52 products on Zephyr (portfolio attached). None was as thin as your sticker, which is exactly why I'd start with the feasibility study.
2. RF antenna, low-power, miniaturized PCB experience:
   My hardware work covers compact and wearable multilayer PCBs, power design and low-power firmware for battery devices: sleep modes, advertising intervals and measuring the real current profile. For a tag this small I'd compare a chip antenna with a tuned PCB trace antenna and check the tuning on the actual surfaces it will stick to. To be straight with you, I'm a BLE product engineer rather than a dedicated antenna specialist, so for final tuning and certification I'd use an RF test lab.
3. Feasibility approach and deliverables:
   Start from your targets (range, battery life, thickness, Find My or your own app). Then check, in order: which thin batteries can handle BLE current peaks, which BLE SoC fits that power budget (likely Nordic nRF52 series), how antenna options behave on common surfaces with a quick dev-board range test, what Find My adds (sound, certification), and the realistic minimum thickness of the stack. Deliverable: a written report with realistic dimensions for a POC and for a production version, a parts and battery shortlist, ranked risks, and a POC plan with cost and timeline.
4. Certifications:
   I don't hold formal RF certifications. For the product itself you'll need Bluetooth SIG qualification, FCC Part 15 (plus CE/UKCA if you sell in Europe) and, if you go that way, Apple's Find My program. I'd map what each one requires in the feasibility report.
5. Recent similar experience:
   Recent work is mostly Nordic nRF52/nRF5340 and ESP32 firmware and boards for connected products, including an audio streaming device on the nRF5340, plus long-running BLE firmware work for clients I've supported for many years.
