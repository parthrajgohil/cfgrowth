## Cover letter
```
Hi,

I read your brief for the LYDIA closet nodes. The electronics work, and now they have to become something people buy and mount in a wardrobe. That step, from a working prototype to a costed, manufacturable board, is most of what my engineering company does.

The first thing I'd settle is how often a battery node reads. A UHF reader draws hundreds of milliamps while it transmits, so runtime depends almost entirely on the read schedule. A battery SmartStrip will probably sleep and read in short bursts on a trigger, like a door switch or an accelerometer, while USB-C and built-in nodes can read all the time. That choice drives the cell, the charging and the enclosure volume, so I'd agree it with your electrical engineer first.

Certification has a similar trap. US UHF RFID runs at 902-928 MHz and Europe at 865-868 MHz, so the CE build needs its own antenna tuning and likely its own SKU. Better to plan that now than find it at the test lab.

I've built connected health devices with their companion apps, and battery-powered BLE products. Before first articles I'd scrub the BOM at your yearly volume rather than the prototype quantity, and check every part's end-of-life date.

One question: which nodes must run on battery, and how long between charges are you aiming for?

I'm in India and can overlap with Pacific mornings every weekday. Happy to talk it through on a short call here on Upwork.

Thanks,
Parthraj
```
