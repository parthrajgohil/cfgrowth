## Cover letter
```
Hi,

For over 10 years I've designed embedded hardware and firmware for clients in more than 10 countries, across 110+ projects, including medical and wearable devices. A handheld waveform generator sits right in that work, from first prototype to production design.

You mentioned the ESP32 or similar, so I'd pick the chip from the waveform needs rather than start from it. The ESP32's DAC is only 8-bit and the newer S3 has none; an STM32 with a 12-bit DAC and hardware timers, or an external DAC/DDS, gives much cleaner waveforms. The ESP32 earns its place if you need Wi-Fi or a phone app, and even then it can sit beside a small MCU that owns the output.

Your screening question mentions TENS, so one thing I'd settle before layout. If the output goes onto a person, the processor shouldn't be the only thing between the patient and the output. I'd put the current limit and an output cut-off in hardware, with their own watchdog, so a firmware hang can't leave the stimulation on. And if you're heading for IEC 60601-2-10, isolation and creepage shape the layout from day one.

Closest work: I built a wearable for a client that fits around the knee and tracks movement, bending and steps, so patients recovering from knee surgery can follow their physical therapy exercises and have their progress monitored. Several of my Upwork client relationships have run for 8+ years.

How I'd start: a short review of your prototype and waveform specs, then the chip choice and schematic, with the output stage proven on the bench before the full layout.

Do you have a prototype or schematic already, and what peak current and voltage do you need at the electrodes?

Happy to talk it through on a short call here on Upwork.

Thanks,
Parthraj
```
