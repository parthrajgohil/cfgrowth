## Cover letter
```
Hi,

A log-first nRF wearable on the lower leg is very close to my own work: I built a knee-worn wearable that tracks movement, bending and steps for patients recovering from knee surgery, and I've worked with Nordic chips (nRF52, nRF5340) for the last 8 years. And yes, I'm happy to sign your NDA before you share the full specs.

The part I'd prove first is the 10 ms target. Over a 120-minute session a typical 20 ppm crystal can drift about 144 ms on its own, so the sync has to come from the system design, not from the BLE link. I'd put a dedicated nRF receiver on the Windows PC that sends a timing beacon, and have every device stamp its samples on its own clock and correct the drift against those beacons. The timestamps then live in the flash log, so the data stays aligned even when a player is out of range for a few minutes.

On hardware, I design compact multilayer PCBs for wearables. I'd size the soldered flash for two hours of IMU data plus margin and check offload speed early, because 30 devices emptying their logs after a session adds up.

How I'd split the milestones: timing and logging proven on three dev kits with the drift measured, then the vest and leg boards designed and brought up, then the dashboard and the 3-device test.

Which IMU and sample rate are you planning for the leg units?

Happy to talk it through on a short call here on Upwork.

Thanks,
Parthraj
```
