## Screening answers
(Submitted with the proposal; already in the preview.)
1. **Bottom-port MEMS mic on the underside:** A through-hole in the PCB centred on the microphone's port and sized per Knowles' guidance, with nothing (no vias, traces or plane edges) in the path. The microphone's ground ring footprint is a complete, unbroken solder ring, because that solder joint is the acoustic seal; a gap there leaks sound around the port. On the top side, a copper-free, flat gasket keep-out ring so the gasket seals evenly. I keep the path through board, gasket and enclosure short, because at your 80 kHz target its resonance matters. Then the electrical side: a filtered microphone supply, an unbroken ground plane under the microphones, and the PDM clock routed away from the data line and the antenna.
2. **GPIOs to avoid on the ESP32-C5-WROOM-1:** The strapping pins first, especially for the WS2812B: the LED's data input or a PDM clock line can pull a strap to the wrong level at reset and change the boot mode. Then the pins the N8R8 module uses internally for flash and PSRAM, UART0 TX/RX (used by the CP2102N for programming and auto-reset), and the USB D-/D+ pins. I'd also keep the JTAG pins free if you want debug access. I take the final pin numbers from the module datasheet's pin table and list them with reasons in the milestone 1 note.
3. **JLCPCB assembled boards:** The checks that save a JLCPCB assembly order: basic versus extended parts (each extended part adds a fee, so I swap them for basic ones where the design allows), live LCSC stock on the day of ordering, and the CPL rotations, which often differ from KiCad's footprint orientation, so I check every part in their placement preview before paying. For your microphones I'd add a handling note: no washing over the open port and the right reflow profile.
