## Screening answers
1. Mains product in production / UL-ETL:
   Power design is part of my regular hardware work, and the closest mains-side project here on Upwork is firmware for an ESP32-C6 relay board. To be straight with you, I haven't led a UL or ETL listing myself, so I won't claim one. For your board I'd design to the creepage, clearance and component ratings of the standards your lab confirms for a plug-in smart plug (typically UL 498A, with UL 60730-1 for the control), use recognised relay, fuse, MOV and supply parts, and have the lab do a pre-compliance review of the schematic before the prototype run.
2. Energy-metering IC for loads below 1 W, and isolation from the ESP32:
   My first choice would be the Cirrus CS5490 (UART, single-phase, built for a wide dynamic range), with the ADI ADE7953 as the alternative. With a shunt, the metering IC sits at line potential, so I'd power it from the line side and pass its UART to the ESP32 through a small two-channel digital isolator. If nothing on the low-voltage side is user-accessible, everything can be referenced to line and the isolator goes away, which is a real cost saving. Either way I'd calibrate each unit at end-of-line test at a low and a high load, because the offset term is what decides standby accuracy.
3. ESP32-C5 or similar, and migration challenges from WROOM-32E:
   Yes, the ESP32-C6 (relay board) and ESP32-H2, alongside classic ESP32 work for years. Moving to the C5: it's a single-core RISC-V chip, so anything pinned to two cores needs reworking; the GPIO map, strapping pins and ADC differ, so pin assignments and boot pins get redone; it needs a recent ESP-IDF 5.x and Arduino support may lag; secure boot and flash-encryption provisioning changes; and on the hardware side the module footprint and antenna placement change, with 5 GHz more sensitive to the enclosure and the relay nearby.
