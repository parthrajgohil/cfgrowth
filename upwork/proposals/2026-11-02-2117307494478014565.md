## Cover letter
```
Hi,

Your timer controller for the 12 V gear motor is close to the work I do most: low-power embedded controllers that sleep for days and then have to do one job reliably. I haven't built a motor timer exactly like this one, but over 10+ years and 110+ projects for clients in 10+ countries most of my devices have run on batteries, from a BLE glucometer to a knee-rehab wearable, where I owned the architecture and firmware. I've also built the HMI and automation for a CNC drilling machine.

The battery version is where this design is won or lost. Unless the pack is 12 V, the motor needs a boost stage, and its quiescent current alone can drain the battery between cycles. I'd put the boost and the motor driver behind a load switch that is fully off in standby, so only the MCU and its RTC are awake, at a few microamps. The other point is the end of the cycle: on battery the motor slows as the cells sag or get cold, so a timed cycle drifts. I'd end on a position switch and keep a timeout only as a stall guard.

For Phase 1 I'd estimate about 40 hours over three weeks from the NDA and motor specs, as a fixed milestone of $2,000. All files and IP are yours.

One question: which battery are you picturing for the battery version, and how long should it last between changes?

Happy to talk it through on a short call here on Upwork.

Thanks,
Parthraj
```
