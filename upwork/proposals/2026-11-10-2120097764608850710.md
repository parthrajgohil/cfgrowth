## Cover letter
```
Hi,

A jar labeling machine you'll build 50 to 100 times a year, with a filling machine next on the same controller, is my kind of project: practical, low cost, and made to be repeated. Machine control is familiar ground for me. My engineering company built the control software and operator screen for a European CNC drilling machine maker, and I'm working on another CNC control application right now.

How I'd solve it, in plain words: a small touchscreen with the computer built in (ESP32-S3) runs both menus. A stepper motor pulls the label strip, because it stops exactly where it's told. One light sensor sees the jar arrive. A second small sensor looks through the label strip and sees the gap between labels, and the machine stops on that gap, not by counting motor turns. Label rolls are never perfectly even, and counting errors add up over a roll. The length and gap the operator enters are used to check the sensor and to set where the label stops at the peel edge.

Rough parts per machine at your volume: touchscreen with ESP32-S3 EUR 30-40, stepper motor and quiet driver EUR 20-25, label gap sensor EUR 10-25, jar sensor EUR 3-8, CE-marked 24 V wall power supply EUR 15-20, wiring, connectors and stop button EUR 15-20. About EUR 95-140 in total. The external power supply also keeps mains voltage out of your machine, which makes the safety side of CE marking simpler.

Prototype: about 70-90 hours over 4-5 weeks. I'd build and test the complete control box on my bench with a sample of your labels, then ship it to you with a wiring diagram and a plain-language build guide.

Are your labels paper or clear film? Clear labels need a different, more expensive gap sensor.

Happy to talk it through on a short call here on Upwork.

Thanks,
Parthraj
```
